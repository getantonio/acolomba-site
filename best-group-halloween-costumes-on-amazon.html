Group-costume questions, answered

How do we pick a group costume that fits everyone?

Look for sets that match your headcount and body types. Multi-person kits like the inflatable caterpillar (#1) scale to different group sizes by adding or removing segments, while apron-based sets (crayons, spices, s’mores) go over everyday clothes in a universal adult size — no per-person sizing stress. If your group is oddly sized, mix-and-match kits (rock band, Super Bros) let each person claim a role that suits them.

What’s usually included in a group costume set?

Most sets on this list include the shared costume pieces for a fixed number of wearers — aprons plus hats, vests plus badge cards, or caps plus mustaches and gloves — but not the base clothing underneath. Plan your base outfits (black shirts, jeans, overalls) to match, and check each listing’s “what’s included” count so every member of your crew actually has a piece.

How do we coordinate a theme without everyone buying the same thing?

Pick a theme with natural variety: the weather-forecast shirts (#10) let each person choose a different forecast, crayon sets (#5) hand out different colors, and the spice set (#7) assigns different condiments. Each person orders their piece separately (or you split one bulk set), and the group photo comes out looking intentional instead of identical.
