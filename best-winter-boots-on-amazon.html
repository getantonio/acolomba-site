Winter boot FAQs

What temperature rating do I actually need?

For most city winters with commuting and errands, a -25°F rating is plenty. Go for -40°F if you live somewhere harsh, spend hours outside, or run cold. The insulation number (200g vs 400g) tells you the warmth bulk — more grams, more warmth, more boot.

How should winter boots fit?

Roomy enough for thick socks with toe wiggle, snug enough that your heel doesn't lift. Try them on late in the day with the socks you'll actually wear — feet swell, and boots don't stretch.

Waterproof vs. water-resistant — does it matter?

Yes. Waterproof means a sealed membrane or bootie that keeps slush out for hours; water-resistant is just a surface treatment that eventually soaks through. Every boot on this list is waterproof.

How do I make winter boots last?

Dry them at room temperature — never on a radiator or vent — and pull removable liners out to air between wears. Wipe salt stains off leather promptly, and re-treat leather uppers each season.
