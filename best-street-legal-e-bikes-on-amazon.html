What "street-legal" means here

Under New York law, an e-bike is a bicycle with operable pedals and a motor rated at under 750 watts:

  • Class 1: pedal-assist only, motor cuts out at 20 MPH
  • Class 2: throttle allowed, cuts out at 20 MPH
  • Class 3 (NYC only): cuts out at 25 MPH — helmet required

Riders must be 16+, and New York City caps e-bike operating speed at 15 MPH. Anything over 750W is legally a moped or motorcycle — registration, plates, and insurance required. Before you buy a street-legal e-bike

What makes an e-bike street-legal?

In New York, an e-bike is a bicycle with operable pedals and a motor rated under 750 watts. Class 1 (pedal-assist) and Class 2 (throttle) cut motor assist at 20 MPH; Class 3 cuts at 25 MPH and is for NYC only. Riders must be 16 or older, and NYC caps e-bike operating speed at 15 MPH. Every bike on this page meets the 750W-and-under, real-pedals test.

Rated watts vs. peak watts — what's the difference?

Rated (nominal) wattage is the motor's continuous legal rating — that's the number the 750W law cares about. Peak wattage is the short burst the motor can hit for a few seconds on a hill. A bike rated 500W with a 1200W peak is still a legal e-bike; a bike rated 1000W is not, even if the listing only brags about the peak number.

Why do some of these show a shipping restriction for NYC?

Amazon restricts e-bike delivery to some addresses based on local laws and its own policies, and New York City has been cracking down on illegal e-moto sellers.
