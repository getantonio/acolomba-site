Ice melt FAQs

How much ice melt do I need for a driveway?

A good rule of thumb: a 25-lb bag covers a standard two-car driveway for several storms, while a 50-lb bag will carry most homes through the whole winter. Start with less than you think — a thin, even layer works better than a pile.

Is ice melt safe for pets and plants?

Some formulas are. Look for products labeled pet-safe — typically magnesium chloride blends or salt-free formulas — and avoid jagged rock salt that can cut paws. Even with pet-safe products, it's smart to wipe your dog's paws after walks, since residue can still irritate.

Calcium chloride vs. rock salt — what's the difference?

Calcium chloride works fast and stays effective to -25°F or colder, generating heat on contact. Plain rock salt (sodium chloride) loses its punch around 15°F and tends to be harsher on concrete and plants. You pay more for calcium chloride, but you use less of it.

Does ice melt damage concrete?

Overuse can — especially repeated freeze-thaw cycles caused by too much product. Use a light, even application, and never use ice melt on new concrete less than a year old, which hasn't fully cured.
